# When Visual Tracking Error Lies: A Reproducible H/B Pilot Benchmark for Screen-Only Aim Behavior Analysis

**Draft status:** working short-paper draft, 2026-11-17  
**Target:** game-security / applied-ML workshop or short paper  
**Claims:** benchmark, measurement validity, negative results; **not** an anti-cheat detector

---

## Abstract

Screen-only analysis of first-person aim behavior is attractive because it requires no
game instrumentation, but small pilot datasets can produce misleadingly high
classification accuracy. We present a protocol-driven pilot benchmark for
human-versus-game-bot (H/B) aim behavior using first-person CS2 recordings. The
benchmark contains 20 balanced sessions (10 H, 10 B) across two maps (`dust2` and
`nuke`) and an additional 6 cross-map H sessions on `aim_botz`. All sessions are
60 seconds, 1920x1080, first-person, offline, and validated with a fail-closed
window-capture protocol and timebase/hash checks. We further audit a lightweight
YOLO detector trained on 320x320 regions of interest (ROI) and show that it
collapses when applied to 1920x1080 first-person tiled frames: head detections
drop from 183/185 (pre-finetune model) to 3/7 (fine-tuned model), despite the
fine-tuned model having better in-domain ROI metrics. Finally, we evaluate five
families of behavior features and show that a tracking-error feature set (F2)
reaches 0.85 accuracy / 0.90 AUC under leave-one-session-out evaluation, but
falls to 0.55 AUC after residualizing against target visibility and target size,
and to 0.60 accuracy on a visibility/size-matched subset. We conclude that
apparent H/B tracking differences in small screen-only datasets are dominated by
measurement confounds, and we distill protocol requirements for future work.

**Keywords:** game security; aim behavior; screen-only capture; benchmark;
measurement validity; negative results

## 1. Introduction

Game-bot detection is often framed as a supervised classification problem. In
practice, collecting labeled cheating data is difficult, and small proxy datasets
can make visually plausible features look more predictive than they are. This
short paper asks a narrower question:

> What can screen-only, first-person aim behavior data actually support?

We study a human-versus-game-bot (H/B) pilot in Counter-Strike 2 (CS2). The
labels are proxy labels: H sessions are played by humans; B sessions are recorded
by an observer following an in-game bot. No real cheat is used, and no game input
is generated by our system. The contribution is therefore not a detector, but a
reproducible benchmark and a measurement study.

The paper makes three contributions:

1. **A fail-closed first-person H/B capture protocol** with POV attestation,
   timebase checks, hash verification, and session-level QC.
2. **A detector domain audit** showing that ROI fine-tuning can improve in-domain
   metrics while collapsing on full-frame first-person tiled inference.
3. **A confound analysis of behavior features** showing that a high-AUC tracking
   feature set is largely explained by target visibility and target size.

The negative result is the main result: in this pilot, visual tracking error is
not a stable behavioral signal once measurement confounds are controlled.

## 3. P0-B Capture Protocol

### 3.1 Sessions

Each session is a 60-second, 1920x1080, first-person recording from the CS2
client window. Capture is screen-only and offline. The protocol requires explicit
attestations for first-person POV and experiment type, and fails closed if the game
window is not foreground, minimized, or otherwise unsafe to capture.

### 3.2 Validation

Every session is checked for:

```text
FILE_INTEGRITY_VERIFIED
VIDEO_DECODE_VERIFIED
GAME_WINDOW_OBSERVED
POV_MANUALLY_ATTESTED
EXPERIMENT_MANUALLY_ATTESTED
TIMEBASE_CONSISTENT
```

Sessions that fail timebase consistency or contain `capture_aborted` are excluded.

### 3.3 Benchmark composition

| Map | H | B | Role |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| dust2 | 5 | 5 | balanced pair |
| nuke | 5 | 5 | balanced pair |
| aim_botz | 6 | 0 | cross-map H only |

The balanced 10H/10B set is used for the main feature evaluation. The 6 aim_botz
H sessions are retained as a cross-map set and are not mixed into H/B training.

## 4. Detector Domain Audit

### 4.1 Models

We compare:

```text
old: runs/detect/cs2_detection_v2/weights/best.onnx
new: runs/detect/cs2_multitarget_ft_v1/weights/best.onnx
```

The new model was fine-tuned on 320x320 ROI crops; the old model was trained on
full-screen 2560x1440 images.

### 4.2 POV inference

Both models are run with 3x3 tiling (tile size 720), `conf=0.35`, `iou=0.5` on
the same first-person sessions.

| Metric | old | new |
|---|---:|---:|
| H head detections | 183 | 3 |
| H selected heads | 132 | 2 |
| B head detections | 185 | 7 |
| B selected heads | 129 | 6 |
| original 90-frame ROI head mAP50 | 0.745 | 0.922 |

The fine-tuned model is better in-domain but collapses on POV frames. We therefore
revert the deployment default to the old model and keep the fine-tuned model as
an experimental branch. This is a domain/scale trade-off, not evidence that the
new weights are corrupt.

**Lesson:** ROI metrics are not a proxy for POV deployment; model selection must
be evaluated on the target capture geometry.

## 5. P0-C Behavior Features

We derive session-level features from old-model tiled inference:

- **F1 acquisition:** delay from target visibility to first selection;
- **F2 tracking:** normalized offset level, variability, slope, settling, jitter;
- **F3 correction:** sign flips, correction magnitude, overshoot, path efficiency;
- **F4 switching:** track/class switches, reacquisition gaps;
- **F5 visibility:** visible fraction, target area, confidence, occlusion gaps.

Feature extraction is episode-aware and avoids raw session length/count features.
Evaluation uses leave-one-session-out (LOO), not frame-level random splits.

### 5.1 F2 result

The fixed F2 set contains:

```text
offset_mean_norm
offset_median_norm
offset_cv
offset_slope
settling_time_mean_s
jitter_ratio
```

Balanced 10H/10B, LOO:

| Model | Accuracy | Balanced | ROC-AUC |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Dummy | 0.500 | 0.500 | 0.500 |
| Logistic | 0.800 | 0.800 | 0.820 |
| RandomForest | 0.850 | 0.850 | 0.900 |

Per-map:

| Map | F2 Logistic Accuracy | F2 Logistic AUC |
|---|---:|---:|
| dust2 | 0.800 | 0.880 |
| nuke | 0.600 | 0.680 |

The per-map gap is an early warning that the pooled result is not stable.

## 6. Confound Analysis

### 6.1 Model comparison

We compare four LOO models:

| Model | Accuracy | ROC-AUC |
|---|---:|---:|
| F2 only | 0.800 | 0.810 |
| Confounders only | 0.600 | 0.780 |
| F2 + confounders | 0.750 | 0.820 |
| F2 residualized | 0.550 | 0.550 |

Confounders:

```text
target_area_median_norm
visible_fraction
target_confidence_mean
visible_duration_median_s
selected_fraction_of_visible
```

Residualizing F2 against these confounders removes nearly all apparent signal.

### 6.2 Scale-invariant features

We add four scale-invariant features:

```text
offset_slope_norm_median
settling_fraction_mean
offset_drop_ratio_mean
offset_iqr_ratio_mean
```

A six-feature invariant set reaches 0.70 accuracy / 0.75 AUC, and 0.50 accuracy /
0.57 AUC after residualization. This does not recover the signal.

### 6.3 Matching

A 1:1 nearest-neighbor match on target area and visible fraction yields 4-6 pairs.
F2 on the matched subset reaches 0.60-0.625 accuracy and 0.75-0.76 AUC. With only
4-6 pairs, this is not evidence of a stable behavioral difference.

### 6.4 Interpretation

The high pooled F2 AUC is best explained by target visibility and target size, not
by H/B behavior. This is the central negative result.

## 7. Lessons for Screen-Only H/B Benchmarks

1. **Map is a first-class confound.** H/B sessions must be paired within each map;
   do not mix map groups across classes.
2. **Visibility and target size must be measured and matched.** Normalizing offset
   by a bounding-box diagonal is not sufficient.
3. **Session-level splits are mandatory.** Frame-level splits leak within-session
   appearance and tracker state.
4. **Model selection must match deployment geometry.** ROI metrics do not transfer
   to full-frame tiled POV inference.
5. **Report negative results.** A high AUC on a small proxy dataset is not evidence
   of anti-cheat capability.
6. **Pre-register behavior features.** Post-hoc feature selection on the same small
   dataset inflates apparent performance.

---

## 8. Limitations

- 10H/10B is small; the cross-map set contains only H sessions.
- One game (CS2) and one scene family (`static_target`).
- H/B are proxy labels; no real cheat is used.
- The detector is a lightweight YOLO model; results may differ for other models.
- Target identity and selection quality come from an automatic tracker and were
  only partially manually QC'd.
- The study is descriptive; no claim of statistical generalization is made.

---

## 9. Ethics and Safety

The system records the game window only. It does not generate mouse or keyboard
input, does not connect to online servers, and does not collect account names,
chat, or voice. Capture fails closed when the game window is not foreground.

## 11. Conclusion

We presented a small, protocol-driven H/B pilot benchmark for screen-only aim
behavior analysis. The benchmark exposes two failure modes that are easy to miss
in small studies: ROI-fine-tuned detectors can collapse on POV frames, and
tracking-error features can achieve high apparent AUC by encoding target
visibility and size. After controlling these confounds, the tracking signal
disappears. We argue that screen-only H/B research should report measurement
confounds and negative results alongside classifier accuracy, and we provide a
protocol and benchmark for doing so.
